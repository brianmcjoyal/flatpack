Crib conversion to toddler bed

Take one side off, add a guardrail and a stabilizer bar. Fifteen minutes of work with a hard age rule attached.

Time30 minutes
PeopleOne is fine
Difficulty●●○○○
Walkthrough3:00 video
Thenre-check it on a schedule

Safety standard: ASTM F1821 (toddler beds)

Once converted, the product is a toddler bed and a different standard applies. The headline rule: never use a toddler bed for a child under 15 months, and most are rated to a maximum of 50 pounds.

Not what you bought? Try Toddler bed and bed rail · Convertible crib · Crib mattress fit and safe sleep check

What you need

In the box already: Stabilizer bar · Conversion rail or guardrail (often sold separately) · Bolts and barrel nuts · Allen wrench

Before you open anything

  • Find the manual first. It is in the pouch sewn to the mattress support, which is exactly why the pouch exists.
  • Confirm you have the right guardrail. Conversion rails are model-specific, and many are sold separately rather than being in the original box.
  • Lower the mattress support to its lowest position before you start, if it is not there already.
  • Decide if your child is ready. Converting early because the crib looks small is the most common reason this goes wrong.

Please don't skip these

  • Never put a child under 15 months in a toddler bed. Openings in and between bed parts can entrap the head and neck of a small child, and infants have died this way.
  • The stabilizer bar is not optional. With one side removed, it is what stops the frame racking. Fit it even if the bed looks fine without it.
  • Do not convert without the correct guardrail for your model. A gap between a guardrail and the mattress is an entrapment hazard.
  • Once converted, the room has to be childproofed. A toddler who can get out of bed can reach everything, so anchor the furniture and cover the outlets before the first night.

The whole thing, step by step

10 steps

  1. Check the age and size rules first

    Fifteen months is the hard floor, and it is a safety limit rather than a suggestion. Most toddler beds are also rated to about fifty pounds.

    • At least 15 months old
    • Under the bed's stated weight limit, usually around 50 pounds
    • Genuinely ready: climbing out of the crib is the real signal, not age alone
    Tip. If your child is climbing out of the crib, convert now — a fall from a crib rail is worse than anything a toddler bed presents.
    Watch out. Converting early to free up the crib for a new baby is understandable and still not safe. Borrow a second crib or use a bassinet instead.

  2. Get the manual out of the pouch

    The booklet is in the pouch stitched to the underside of the mattress support. Conversion steps are at the back, in their own section.

    • Lift the mattress and look under the support
    • Find the conversion section
    • Check which parts the conversion needs and whether you have them
    Tip. If the booklet is gone, every major manufacturer has it by model number on their site. The model is on the label on the crib end's bottom rail.

  3. Lower the mattress support

    The mattress support has to be at its lowest position for a toddler bed. If it is higher, undo the four corner bolts and drop it now.

    • Four corner bolts out
    • Support down to the lowest setting
    • All four bolts back in and fully tightened
    Watch out. A toddler bed at crib height is a fall hazard. This step is easy to skip because the crib is already built and the mattress is already in.

  4. Remove one side — gently

    Undo the four bolts on one long side and lift it away. Pull straight out rather than levering, so you do not damage the holes you will bolt back into.

    • Four bolts out, keeping the barrel nuts if they come loose
    • Lift the side straight away from the frame
    • Bag every bolt and barrel nut and label the bag
    Tip. Keep the removed side and all its hardware. You will want them if you sell the crib or have another baby.
    Watch out. Store the hardware with the side panel, not loose in a drawer. Conversion hardware going missing is the most common reason a crib cannot be converted back.

  5. Fit the stabilizer bar

    The stabilizer bar runs along the open side, low down, and bolts to both ends. It replaces the structural job the removed side was doing.

    • Line it up with the pin holes at both ends
    • Barrel nuts rotated and held with the screwdriver
    • Both bolts started by hand, then tightened firmly
    Tip. Pull gently at both ends as you seat it — the manual actually tells you to, because the pins need to engage before the bolts go in.
    Watch out. Without the stabilizer bar the frame can rack side to side under a child's weight. Fit it even on a daybed configuration.

  6. Fit the guardrail and close the gap

    The guardrail bolts to the open side. Once it is on, check there is no gap between the rail and the mattress that a child could slip into.

    • Bolt the rail at both ends, fully tight
    • Push the mattress firmly against the rail
    • Check for gaps along the whole length — you should not be able to fit more than two fingers
    Tip. If the gap is too big, the mattress is likely too small for the frame. Use a full-size crib mattress, not a smaller one.
    Watch out. A gap between the guardrail and the mattress is an entrapment hazard, which is the exact mechanism the 15-month rule exists to guard against.

  7. Check the mattress size against the toddler rules

    A toddler bed needs a full-size crib mattress: at least fifty-one and five-eighths inches long, twenty-seven and a quarter wide, four inches thick, and no more than six inches thick.

    • Measure length, width and thickness
    • At least 4 inches thick, never more than 6
    • Two-finger test around every edge
    Watch out. Too thick and the guardrail is effectively shorter than designed. Too thin and the gaps open up. Both ends of that range matter.

  8. Full re-tighten of everything

    You have just disturbed a frame that was holding tension. Go around every remaining bolt on the whole bed and bring it up firm. Two laps.

    • Every bolt on both ends, the remaining side, the stabilizer bar and the guardrail
    • Mattress support bolts underneath as well
    • Second lap after the first
    Tip. Wood threads lose a little grip each time a crib comes apart. A converted crib is on at least its second assembly, so it deserves more attention than a new one, not less.

  9. Childproof the room before the first night

    The night your child can get out of bed is the night every piece of furniture in the room becomes climbable. Do this before bedtime, not after.

    • Anchor the dresser, bookcase and any shelving to the wall
    • Cords from blinds tied up and out of reach
    • Outlet covers, and a gate at the door or the top of the stairs if needed
    Watch out. The conversion and the childproofing are one job, not two. A toddler bed without a childproofed room has just removed the only thing that was containing them overnight.

  10. Set the re-check reminder

    A toddler bed takes far more abuse than a crib — it gets climbed on, bounced on, and sat on the edge of. Monthly checks from here.

    • Monthly reminder to re-tighten every bolt
    • Monthly check of the guardrail-to-mattress gap
    • Check the stabilizer bar is still firm
    Tip. Keep the Allen wrench taped under the bed frame. You will reach for it more often than you expect.

Keep checking it

5 checks

Manuals tell you to inspect "periodically" and never say how often or what to look at. This is that missing schedule. Fasteners lose their grip gradually — CPSC testing found a crib bolt backed off half a turn has already lost its clamping force — so the checks matter more than the build.

Every month
Re-tighten every bolt on the frame, the stabilizer bar and the guardrail. Toddler beds take a lot of dynamic load.
Every month
Push the mattress against the guardrail and re-run the two-finger gap check. Mattresses shift.
Every month
Rack the frame side to side by the headboard. If it moves, the stabilizer bar bolts have loosened.
Every three months
Re-check that every piece of furniture in the room is still anchored.
When your child nears the weight limit
Most toddler beds stop at around 50 pounds. Check the label and plan the move to a twin bed.

If something has gone wrong

I do not have the conversion rail

Conversion rails are model-specific and usually sold separately. Find your model number on the label on the crib end's bottom rail and order the matching rail from the manufacturer. Do not improvise one.

The bolt holes do not line up for the stabilizer bar

Pull gently at both ends of the bar as the manual describes — the locating pins need to seat before the bolts will reach. Also loosen the bolts at the other end so the frame can shuffle.

There is a gap between the guardrail and the mattress

Push the mattress hard against the rail. If a gap remains, your mattress is undersized for the frame. A full-size crib mattress is a regulated size — measure yours against it.

The bed racks side to side

The stabilizer bar is missing, loose, or fitted to only one end. That bar is doing the structural work the removed side used to do.

My child is 13 months and climbing out of the crib

Do not convert yet. Instead drop the mattress to its lowest setting if it is not already, remove anything in the crib that can be used as a step, and consider a sleep sack that makes climbing harder. If they are genuinely climbing out, a floor mattress is safer than a toddler bed before 15 months.

Can I convert back to a crib later?

Yes, if you kept the side panel and its hardware. Reassemble, fully re-tighten everything, and re-run the slat and mattress gap checks — a crib on its third assembly needs a careful going-over.

Words you will see in the manual

barrel nut
A metal cylinder that drops into a round hole in a crib or bed frame, with a threaded hole running across it. The bolt comes in from the side and threads into that cross hole. If the cylinder is rotated even slightly, the bolt cannot find the thread — turn the slot on its end with a flat-head screwdriver until you can see straight through it.
stabilizer bar
The bar fitted along the open side when a crib converts to a toddler bed. It does the structural job the removed side used to do and is not optional.
two finger test
Push the mattress hard into one corner, then try to slide two flat adult fingers into the gap on the opposite side. If they fit easily, the mattress is too small for the frame. The most important five seconds in a nursery.
mattress support
The frame, springs or board that holds a crib mattress up, bolted at all four corners. It is the most repeated structural failure point in cribs, and because it sits under the mattress you never see it unless you look on purpose.
anti tip
A strap or bracket that ties furniture to the wall so it cannot fall forward. Required on anything taller than it is wide, and on every dresser in a home with children.
