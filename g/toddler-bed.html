Toddler bed and bed rail

A low frame with guard rails, or a rail added to a full-size bed. The gap between rail and mattress is the thing to get right.

Check this model for a recall first

Nursery products are recalled more often than any other furniture category, and a recall can be issued years after a product ships. Find the model number on the product label, then search it at cpsc.gov/recalls. Register the product with the manufacturer too — that registration is the only way they can reach you later. This matters most for anything bought second-hand or handed down.

Safety standard: ASTM F1821 (toddler beds) / ASTM F2085 (bed rails)

Toddler beds are for children at least 15 months old and typically up to about 50 pounds. Portable bed rails are a separate product with their own standard, and they are intended for children roughly 2 to 5 years old — never for infants, and never on a crib.

What you need

In the box already: Allen key · Bolts and barrel nuts · Guard rails · Slats · Bed rail straps and brackets

Before you open anything

  • Confirm your child is at least 15 months old. Below that, a toddler bed is not a safe product regardless of how big they are.
  • Measure the mattress you intend to use before you build. Toddler beds take a full-size crib mattress, not a twin.
  • Childproof the room first. A child who can get out of bed can reach everything in the room, and that change happens on night one.

Please don't skip these

  • Never use a toddler bed for a child under 15 months. Openings in and between bed parts can entrap the head and neck of a small child.
  • A portable bed rail must never be used on a crib, a toddler bed with its own rails, or for a child under about two years old. The hazard is entrapment between the rail and the mattress.
  • There must be no gap between a bed rail and the mattress that a child could roll into. This is the mechanism behind the fatalities associated with bed rails.
  • Anchor every piece of furniture in the room. The night the bed goes in is the night the room stops containing them.

The whole thing, step by step

10 steps

  1. Check the age, weight and recall

    Fifteen months minimum, and most toddler beds stop at around fifty pounds. Check the model against the recall list while you are at it.

    • At least 15 months old
    • Under the stated weight limit, usually around 50 pounds
    • Model number searched at cpsc.gov/recalls
    Watch out. Bed rails have their own, higher floor: roughly two years old. A rail on a bed with a younger child is the hazard, not the protection.

  2. Lay out the parts and sort the hardware

    Two ends, two sides with integrated guard rails, slats and a hardware bag. Count everything against the manual first.

    • Match every panel to its letter
    • Separate the bolt lengths — they are not interchangeable
    • Check for barrel nuts and the flat-head screwdriver you will need to rotate them
    Tip. If this frame uses barrel nuts, the same alignment trick from crib assembly applies: rotate the slot with a screwdriver so the bolt can find the thread.

  3. Bolt the frame together loose

    Attach both sides to both ends with every bolt started by hand and nothing tightened. The frame needs slack to square itself up.

    • All four corners started by hand
    • Washers under every bolt head
    • Nothing tightened yet
    Watch out. If a bolt will not start by hand, back it out. Cross-threading destroys the insert.

  4. Square the frame and tighten

    Measure both diagonals and push the long corner until they match. Then tighten everything in two full laps.

    • Diagonals equal
    • Tighten from the center outward
    • Second lap after the first
    Tip. A square frame is what keeps the mattress sitting tight against the rails. Out-of-square frames are where gaps come from.

  5. Fit and secure every slat

    All slats in, evenly spaced, and each one clipped or screwed down. A toddler bed gets jumped on, so no slat is optional.

    • Count the slats against the manual
    • Even spacing along the frame
    • Every slat secured, not just the end ones
    Watch out. An unsecured slat can be pushed out from below. Collapsing slats are a documented failure mode in children's beds.

  6. Fit the guard rails

    Bolt the guard rails on fully tight, then push and pull on them hard. They are going to take a child's full weight leaning on them.

    • Both rails bolted at every point
    • Tightened fully, then pull hard on each
    • Check the rail height above the mattress is what the manual specifies
    Tip. Most toddler beds put a rail on part of each side, leaving an opening to get in and out. Check that opening is where you want it before tightening.

  7. Check the mattress size and the gaps

    A toddler bed takes a full-size crib mattress: at least fifty-one and five-eighths by twenty-seven and a quarter inches, between four and six inches thick. Then test every gap.

    • Measure the mattress against those dimensions
    • Push it tight against one rail and two-finger test the opposite side and both ends
    • Check the gap between the rail and the mattress along its whole length
    Watch out. Too thick a mattress makes the rail effectively shorter. Too thin or too small and gaps open up beside it. Both directions matter.

  8. Bed rails on a full-size bed: strap them properly

    If you are adding a portable rail to a regular bed, the straps must run under the mattress and be pulled tight, with the rail hard against the mattress edge.

    • Straps under the mattress, not just the sheets, and pulled tight
    • Rail pressed firmly against the mattress with no gap
    • Push the rail away from the bed hard — no gap should open
    Watch out. The fatal mechanism with bed rails is a child rolling into the gap between the rail and the mattress and becoming trapped. A loose rail is more dangerous than no rail at all.

  9. Childproof the room before the first night

    Anchor the dresser, the bookcase and anything else climbable. Tie up blind cords. Fit a gate if the stairs are a risk.

    • Every piece of furniture anchored to a stud
    • Blind and curtain cords tied up out of reach
    • Gate at the stairs, outlet covers, nothing climbable near a window
    Watch out. The bed and the childproofing are one job. A toddler bed in an unprepared room has simply removed the barrier that was keeping them in.

  10. Set the re-check reminder

    Toddler beds get bounced on, climbed on and sat on the edge of, every day. Monthly checks from here.

    • Monthly reminder to re-tighten every bolt
    • Monthly check of the rail-to-mattress gap
    • Keep the Allen key taped under the frame
    Tip. A bed that creaks is a bed with a loose bolt. Treat the creak as the reminder.

Keep checking it

6 checks

Manuals tell you to inspect "periodically" and never say how often or what to look at. This is that missing schedule. Fasteners lose their grip gradually — CPSC testing found a crib bolt backed off half a turn has already lost its clamping force — so the checks matter more than the build.

Every month
Re-tighten every bolt in the frame and both guard rails. Children's beds take far more dynamic load than adult beds.
Every month
Push the mattress against each rail and re-run the two-finger gap check along the whole length.
Every month, for a portable bed rail
Re-tighten the under-mattress straps and confirm no gap opens when you push the rail away from the bed.
Every month
Check every slat is still secured and none has cracked.
Every three months
Re-check that all the furniture in the room is still anchored.
When your child nears the weight limit
Most toddler beds stop at around 50 pounds. Check the label and plan the move to a twin bed.

If something has gone wrong

There is a gap between the bed rail and the mattress

Tighten the under-mattress straps and push the rail hard against the mattress. If a gap remains, the rail is the wrong length for the bed or the mattress is too small. Do not use it with a gap — that is the exact mechanism behind bed rail fatalities.

My child is 14 months and climbing out of the crib

Do not move them to a toddler bed yet. Drop the crib mattress to the lowest setting, remove anything that can be used as a step, and consider a sleep sack. A floor mattress is a safer interim option than a toddler bed before 15 months.

The bed creaks and rocks

Loosen every bolt, square the frame by its diagonals on a flat floor, and retighten in two laps. Check the slats are all secured too — unsecured slats shifting is a common creak source.

Can I use a twin mattress in a toddler bed?

No. Toddler beds are sized for a full-size crib mattress. A twin is both too large to fit and will not sit correctly against the rails.

Can I put a bed rail on the toddler bed as well?

No. Portable bed rails are designed for a standard bed with a child of about two to five. Adding one to a toddler bed that already has integrated rails creates new gaps between the two rails.

My child fell out of the open side

Many toddler beds leave an entry gap by design. Position the bed with that opening against a wall, or add a soft rug or crash mat on that side. Some models sell a second rail to close it.

Words you will see in the manual

barrel nut
A metal cylinder that drops into a round hole in a crib or bed frame, with a threaded hole running across it. The bolt comes in from the side and threads into that cross hole. If the cylinder is rotated even slightly, the bolt cannot find the thread — turn the slot on its end with a flat-head screwdriver until you can see straight through it.
two finger test
Push the mattress hard into one corner, then try to slide two flat adult fingers into the gap on the opposite side. If they fit easily, the mattress is too small for the frame. The most important five seconds in a nursery.
slat
One of the wooden boards that spans a bed frame to support the mattress. Gaps wider than about three inches will make a foam mattress sag.
square
Describes a frame whose corners are true right angles. You check it by measuring both diagonals: if the two numbers match, it is square.
anti tip
A strap or bracket that ties furniture to the wall so it cannot fall forward. Required on anything taller than it is wide, and on every dresser in a home with children.
stud
The vertical timber inside a wall, usually every 16 or 24 inches. It is what you screw an anti-tip bracket into. Drywall alone will not hold furniture.
