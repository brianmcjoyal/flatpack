Assembly guide

Metal platform bed frame

Steel side rails, bolt-on legs, and a roll of slats. No box spring needed.

What you need

In the box already: Allen key · Hex bolts · Washers · Center support legs · Slat roll or individual slats

Before you open anything

  • Build it in the bedroom. An assembled queen frame does not fit through a standard door.
  • Have the mattress nearby but leaning against a wall, not on the floor where you need to work.
  • Lay a towel or the cardboard down. Steel rails scratch hardwood instantly.

Please don't skip these

  • Steel edges from the factory can be sharp. Wipe the oil off and watch your fingers on cut ends.
  • Never skip the center support legs on a queen or king. That is what stops the frame from folding in the middle.

The whole thing, step by step

9 steps

  1. Unpack and sort into three piles

    Make three piles. Long rails, short rails and legs, and the hardware bag. Wipe the oil off the steel now rather than onto your sheets later.

    • Two long side rails
    • Two short head and foot rails, plus legs
    • One hardware bag: bolts, washers, Allen key
    Tip. The hardware bag is often taped inside one of the tubes. Shake them if you cannot find it.

  2. Lay the rectangle out on the floor first

    Put the four rails on the floor in the shape of the bed before you attach anything. Long rails on the sides, short ones at the head and foot.

    • Form the rectangle roughly where the bed will live
    • Point the bracket holes inward and up
    • Check the head rail is the one with the headboard bolt holes, if your frame has them
    Watch out. Head and foot rails can look identical but only one has the extra holes for a headboard. Compare them side by side.

  3. Bolt the corners finger-tight only

    Put every corner bolt in with its washer, but only finger-tight for now. Leaving them loose lets the frame shuffle into square.

    • Washer always goes between the bolt head and the steel
    • Thread each bolt in by hand several turns
    • Four corners, loose
    Tip. Tightening as you go is why frames end up with one bolt that will not reach its hole. Loose first, tight last. Every single time.
    Watch out. If a bolt will not start by hand, you are cross-threading it. Back it out and start again straight.

  4. Fit the center support bar

    Queen and king frames have a bar that runs down the middle, head to foot. It bolts to the head and foot rails. This is the backbone.

    • Run it from head rail to foot rail through the center
    • Bolt both ends finger-tight
    • If it comes in two halves, join them in the middle first
    Watch out. Some frames have a second cross bar. Check the manual for how many — missing one is the usual cause of a frame that sags under a mattress.

  5. Screw the legs in

    Legs either thread into a socket or bolt onto a plate. Get them all in, including the center legs, which are the ones that actually carry the weight.

    • Thread each leg in until it stops
    • Center legs go on the support bar
    • Count them against the manual: there are usually more than you expect
    Tip. If a leg has a plastic foot, make sure it is pressed fully on. They fall off and then the frame rocks.

  6. Square it, then tighten everything

    Measure both diagonals of the rectangle and nudge until they match. Now go around and tighten every bolt firmly. Corners first, then the center.

    • Measure corner to corner both ways
    • Push the long corner in until the numbers match
    • Tighten all bolts in a circuit, then do a second lap
    Tip. A second lap matters: tightening one bolt loosens its neighbor. Two laps and it is solid.

  7. Roll out the slats

    Unroll the slats across the frame and space them evenly. Most have straps and a screw or clip at each end to stop them sliding.

    • Unroll from head to foot
    • Space them evenly along the frame
    • Secure the end slats with the screws or clips provided
    Watch out. Slats that are not fixed down creep toward the foot of the bed over a few months and then you have a gap under your hips.

  8. Shake it hard before the mattress goes on

    Grab a corner and rock it firmly. Any knock or squeak is a loose bolt, and it is ten times easier to find now than at midnight.

    • Push and rock each corner
    • Listen for a metallic knock
    • Chase the noise and tighten that bolt
    Tip. Most squeaky metal bed complaints are one under-tightened bolt or a missing washer, not a bad frame.

  9. Mattress on, then set it against the wall

    Walk the mattress on from the foot end. Then slide the whole bed into place and check it sits level and does not rock.

    • Lift from the foot, not the side
    • Center it on the slats
    • Slide into position and check for rocking on uneven floors
    Tip. If it rocks on an uneven floor, a folded piece of cardboard under one leg fixes it permanently and costs nothing.

If something has gone wrong

The last bolt hole will not line up

Classic sign you tightened as you went. Loosen every other bolt a couple of turns, the frame will shuffle, the bolt will go in, then re-tighten everything.

The bed squeaks when I move

Do a full tightening lap. If it still squeaks, the usual culprits are a missing washer, slats rubbing on the rail — put felt pads or a strip of fabric under the slat ends — or the frame touching the wall.

The center sags under the mattress

A center support leg is missing or not touching the floor. Check every leg contacts the ground; some thread in and out to adjust height.

There are leftover bolts

Check for a second cross support or extra legs you skipped, and check whether your frame also supports a headboard. Manufacturers do include a few spares, but count against the manual before assuming yours are spare.

My frame came with no slats

Some platform frames are designed for a bunkie board or a bed base instead. Check the listing. Putting a mattress straight onto bare rails voids most mattress warranties.

Words you will see in the manual

slat
One of the wooden boards that spans a bed frame to support the mattress. Gaps wider than about three inches will make a foam mattress sag.
center support
The bar running down the middle of a bed frame, head to foot, with its own legs. It carries most of the load and is not optional on a queen or larger.
washer
A flat metal ring that goes under a bolt head. It spreads the load so the bolt does not pull through the material. Never leave one out.
square
Describes a frame whose corners are true right angles. You check it by measuring both diagonals: if the two numbers match, it is square.
