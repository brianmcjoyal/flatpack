Something's gone wrong

178 things that go wrong mid-build, and what to actually do about each one.

Storage

The cam lock just spins forever and never tightens

The bolt head is not inside the cam. Pull the joint apart and look at the cam from the side: the keyhole gap must point at the edge the bolt comes from. Turn the cam around and try again. Full guide: Cam-lock box furniture →

The holes do not line up

Almost always a panel is flipped or in the wrong position. Pull it apart, lay both panels down matching the manual's picture exactly, and compare hole positions before rejoining. Full guide: Cam-lock box furniture →

A screw hole is stripped and the screw spins

Break a wooden toothpick or two off inside the hole with a drop of wood glue, let it set twenty minutes, then screw into that. Holds surprisingly well. Full guide: Cam-lock box furniture →

The whole thing wobbles or leans

The back panel is either missing nails or was fitted while the box was out of square. Pull a few nails, square the box with the diagonal measurement, re-nail. Full guide: Cam-lock box furniture →

A part is missing or damaged

Go to the manufacturer's site and search 'replacement parts'. IKEA, Sauder, Ameriwood, South Shore and most others ship hardware free. Have your model number and the part letter from the manual ready. Full guide: Cam-lock box furniture →

I put it together in the wrong order and now I am stuck

Cam locks are designed to come apart. Turn them counterclockwise half a turn and the panels separate. Nothing is ruined. Go back two steps. Full guide: Cam-lock box furniture →

The drawer will not close flush

Usually one slide is set further back than the other, or is not level. Loosen both slides on that drawer, push the drawer closed, hold the front flush, and retighten. Full guide: Drawers and drawer slides →

The drawer is crooked or drops at one side

The two slides are at different heights. Measure from the cabinet bottom to each slide. They must match. Fix the one that is wrong, not both. Full guide: Drawers and drawer slides →

The drawer scrapes or grinds

Either the drawer box is out of square, the cabinet is out of square, or a screw head is standing proud and catching. Run your finger along the slide path to feel for a snag. Full guide: Drawers and drawer slides →

A slide screw stripped its hole

Toothpick plus wood glue into the hole, let it set, re-drive. Or move to one of the slide's other holes, which exist for exactly this reason. Full guide: Drawers and drawer slides →

The drawer falls out if I pull it all the way

Drawer stops are missing or not fitted. Check the hardware bag for small plastic clips. If they are genuinely absent, request them from the manufacturer. Full guide: Drawers and drawer slides →

The drawer base keeps popping out of its groove

The box was assembled out of square, or the base is nailed in only at one end. Square the box with the diagonals, then nail or screw the base at every corner. Full guide: Drawers and drawer slides →

The bookcase leans forward at the top

The frame was nailed out of square, or the floor slopes. Loosen a few back panel nails, square the diagonals, re-nail. Then anchor it to the wall, which also pulls the top back into line. Full guide: Bookcase or shelving unit →

A shelf sags in the middle

Too much weight for the span. Turn the shelf over so it bows the other way, move heavy items to the bottom, or add a small support block under the center. Wide bookcases are simply weak in the middle. Full guide: Bookcase or shelving unit →

The shelf rocks when I touch it

One shelf pin is in the wrong hole. Count holes up from the bottom at all four corners — the counts must be identical. Full guide: Bookcase or shelving unit →

The whole thing twists if I push a corner

The back panel is doing its job badly: either missing nails, or not nailed into the fixed shelves. Add nails along every edge and every fixed shelf. Full guide: Bookcase or shelving unit →

A cam lock snapped

They are a standard size, sold in packs at any hardware store or online as '15mm cam lock fittings'. Pick out the broken one and press in a new one. Full guide: Bookcase or shelving unit →

A plastic connector cracked

They are sold separately by every manufacturer and in generic multi-packs. Order spares — if one cracked, the unit is probably under some stress, so check the grid is square. Full guide: Cube organizer and modular shelving →

The whole unit racks and leans like a parallelogram

That is normal for a backless cube unit and exactly what the wall strap is for. Square it, then anchor it. Adding a back panel of thin hardboard also works very well. Full guide: Cube organizer and modular shelving →

The panels will not push into the connectors

You are going in at an angle, or there is a burr in the slot. Line it up dead square and press with your palm, not your fingertips. Check the slot for flashing you can trim with a knife. Full guide: Cube organizer and modular shelving →

A cube is slightly too small for my bins

Measure the cube opening and buy bins to match. Cube sizes vary between brands even though they look standard — a common size is around thirteen inches, but not universally. Full guide: Cube organizer and modular shelving →

Can I stack two units?

Only if the manufacturer says so and only when both are anchored to the wall. An unanchored stack is the least stable furniture arrangement in a house. Full guide: Cube organizer and modular shelving →

A shelf sits crooked

The sleeves are at different groove numbers. Lift the shelf off, count the grooves on all four posts from the bottom, and set them all identical. Full guide: Wire or metal utility shelving →

The shelf will not go down onto the sleeve

A sleeve is upside down. They are tapered — the wide end goes up so the shelf collar can wedge onto it. Full guide: Wire or metal utility shelving →

I need to change a shelf height

Lift off every shelf above it, move the sleeves, and rebuild upward. It is quick but there is no shortcut, which is why choosing heights first matters. Full guide: Wire or metal utility shelving →

The rack sways side to side

The top shelf is missing or not at the very top of the posts. The top shelf is the brace. Also confirm every shelf corner is fully tapped down. Full guide: Wire or metal utility shelving →

The posts came in two halves

Many tall racks use threaded post sections that screw together to make a full-height post, or they can be used as two short racks. Both are correct — pick one before you start. Full guide: Wire or metal utility shelving →

The whole frame leans or racks like a parallelogram

Joints are not fully seated, or the internal velcro ties that hold the cover to the frame are not fastened. Those ties are structural. Push every joint home and fasten them all. Full guide: Fabric wardrobe or portable closet →

The hanging rail sags or fell down

Overloaded, or the rail was only seated at one end. Reduce the weight and make sure both ends are fully pushed into their connectors. Full guide: Fabric wardrobe or portable closet →

A plastic connector cracked

Replacements are sold by the manufacturer and as generic multi-packs. If a connector cracked, the frame is under more load than it should be — reduce the weight too. Full guide: Fabric wardrobe or portable closet →

A tube keeps falling out of its connector

The fit has loosened. Wrap a couple of turns of electrical tape around the tube end and push it back in. It will hold indefinitely. Full guide: Fabric wardrobe or portable closet →

The cover will not zip up

The frame is out of square, so the cover is being stretched diagonally. Square up the base rectangle and the zip will usually close easily. Full guide: Fabric wardrobe or portable closet →

It will not fit in the space I measured

Check whether you used the right tubes for depth and width. Swapping a depth tube for a width tube changes the footprint and is easy to do when the lengths are close. Full guide: Fabric wardrobe or portable closet →

Bedroom

The last bolt hole will not line up

Classic sign you tightened as you went. Loosen every other bolt a couple of turns, the frame will shuffle, the bolt will go in, then re-tighten everything. Full guide: Metal platform bed frame →

The bed squeaks when I move

Do a full tightening lap. If it still squeaks, the usual culprits are a missing washer, slats rubbing on the rail — put felt pads or a strip of fabric under the slat ends — or the frame touching the wall. Full guide: Metal platform bed frame →

The center sags under the mattress

A center support leg is missing or not touching the floor. Check every leg contacts the ground; some thread in and out to adjust height. Full guide: Metal platform bed frame →

There are leftover bolts

Check for a second cross support or extra legs you skipped, and check whether your frame also supports a headboard. Manufacturers do include a few spares, but count against the manual before assuming yours are spare. Full guide: Metal platform bed frame →

My frame came with no slats

Some platform frames are designed for a bunkie board or a bed base instead. Check the listing. Putting a mattress straight onto bare rails voids most mattress warranties. Full guide: Metal platform bed frame →

I cannot find the bolt holes on the headboard

They are behind the fabric. Lift any flap along the bottom edge, or feel for a hard metal plate and part the fabric with your fingers. Check the manual diagram for roughly how far from the edge. Never cut. Full guide: Upholstered or wooden bed with a headboard →

The headboard wobbles side to side

The headboard bolts are not tight, or washers were left out. Washers spread the load and stop the bolt pulling through. Take it apart far enough to add them. Full guide: Upholstered or wooden bed with a headboard →

The side rails are different and I bolted on the wrong one

Easy fix. Loosen it off, swap sides, re-bolt. Check inside faces for an L or R sticker. Full guide: Upholstered or wooden bed with a headboard →

There is a gap between the mattress and the headboard

The frame is out of square, or the slats have drifted toward the foot. Square the diagonals and push the slat set back up to the head. Full guide: Upholstered or wooden bed with a headboard →

The bed creaks every time I move

Full tightening lap first. Then check for wood-on-wood contact at the slat ends and stick on felt pads. Also make sure the frame is not touching the wall — that knock sounds exactly like a frame creak. Full guide: Upholstered or wooden bed with a headboard →

The frame wobbles side to side

Bolts are not tight enough, which is very common because an Allen key cannot generate enough torque. Go around every bolt with a socket wrench or wrench. Also confirm the diagonals are square and all slats are fitted and secured. Full guide: Bunk bed or loft bed →

A bolt hole does not line up

Loosen every bolt on that bunk a couple of turns. The frame will shuffle and the bolt will go in. Then retighten everything from the center outward. Never drill a new hole in a bunk bed. Full guide: Bunk bed or loft bed →

I am missing a bolt

Stop and order the exact part from the manufacturer. Do not substitute a hardware store bolt — length and grade are engineered for this load. Every major brand ships bunk bed hardware free and quickly. Full guide: Bunk bed or loft bed →

The guard rail gap looks too big with our mattress

Your mattress is thicker than the bed was designed for. Use a thinner one. This is the single most common bunk bed safety failure and no amount of tightening fixes it. Full guide: Bunk bed or loft bed →

The bed creaks when my child moves

Do a full wrench lap. Then check for wood-on-wood contact at the slat ends and add felt pads. Also make sure the bed is not touching the wall — that knock is often mistaken for a frame creak. Full guide: Bunk bed or loft bed →

Can I separate it into two single beds?

Only if the manual says so and only using the parts supplied for it. Many bunks convert, but some posts are not designed to be bed legs on their own. Full guide: Bunk bed or loft bed →

It is not expanding to full height

Give it forty-eight hours in a warm room. Cold foam expands slowly, so if it arrived off a cold truck it can take longer. Walking gently on it also helps the cells open. Full guide: Mattress in a box →

It smells strongly

That is off-gassing and it is normal and harmless. Leave it bare with a window open and a fan running for a day or two. Full guide: Mattress in a box →

I cut into the foam

A shallow nick in the cover is cosmetic and will not affect the mattress. A deep cut into the foam cannot be repaired. Contact the retailer, but be aware opening damage is often excluded. Full guide: Mattress in a box →

There is a dip where I sleep after a few months

Check the base before blaming the mattress. Slat gaps wider than about three inches, or a missing center support, will cause exactly this and are usually the real culprit. Full guide: Mattress in a box →

Can I put it back in the box to return it?

No. Home compression is not possible. Almost every brand with a sleep trial arranges collection or donation instead — check the policy rather than trying to re-roll it. Full guide: Mattress in a box →

Office

The chair sinks down slowly when I sit on it

The gas cylinder has failed. It is not repairable but it is a cheap, universal part — almost all office chairs use the same size. Search 'office chair gas cylinder replacement' and swap it out. Full guide: Office chair with a gas lift →

The seat will not stay on the cylinder

Sit on it properly. It is a tapered friction fit that needs real weight to seat. If it still lifts off, check the cylinder is dropped fully into the base. Full guide: Office chair with a gas lift →

The back leans to one side

One side's bolts are tighter than the other, or a washer was missed. Loosen all the back bolts, hold it straight, and tighten evenly in a cross pattern. Full guide: Office chair with a gas lift →

A caster keeps falling out

It was never fully seated. Lay the base on its side, line the stem up straight, and hit it once with a rubber mallet. If the socket itself is cracked, the base needs replacing. Full guide: Office chair with a gas lift →

The height lever is on the wrong side

The mechanism is bolted on backwards. Undo the four bolts under the seat, turn it 180 degrees, re-bolt. Full guide: Office chair with a gas lift →

It rolls away on hardwood floors

Standard office casters are made for carpet. Swap in rubber or polyurethane casters — they pull out and push in, no tools, and they cost very little. Full guide: Office chair with a gas lift →

I cannot find where the back attaches

Under the zipped or velcroed flaps on both sides of the seat. The metal brackets are often folded flat against the seat and need their bolts loosened so they can swing upright. Full guide: Gaming or racing chair →

The back leans to one side

One side was tightened before the other. Loosen all the side bolts, hold the back straight while looking from the front, and tighten alternating sides a little at a time. Full guide: Gaming or racing chair →

The chair sinks when I sit on it

Failed gas cylinder. It is a standard, inexpensive, universal part on nearly all chairs. Search for an office chair gas cylinder replacement in the same size class. Full guide: Gaming or racing chair →

The recline lever does nothing

Check the tilt tension knob underneath — wound fully tight, the back will not move. Loosen it several turns and try again. Full guide: Gaming or racing chair →

A bolt will not go all the way in

Wrong length from the wrong bag, or the bracket is not quite aligned. Compare it to the manual's chart, and loosen the other bolts so the bracket can shift. Full guide: Gaming or racing chair →

The armrests are at different heights

They are swapped left for right. Unbolt, swap, rebolt. Full guide: Gaming or racing chair →

The desk wobbles however much I tighten it

Three usual causes: the crossbar is missing or loose, the floor is uneven and the feet need adjusting, or a leg plate screw is stripped. Check in that order. Full guide: Four-leg desk or table →

A screw went through the top

It was the wrong length. Back it out, fill the hole with a wax touch-up stick or colored filler, and use the correct short screw. Compare lengths before driving the rest. Full guide: Four-leg desk or table →

A leg plate screw spins and will not bite

Stripped hole. Push a wooden toothpick or two with a drop of wood glue into the hole, let it set, then re-drive. Or move to a fresh hole a half inch away if the plate allows. Full guide: Four-leg desk or table →

The desk is not level even though it does not rock

Rock and level are different problems. Use a level on the top and adjust the feet on the low side until the bubble centers. Full guide: Four-leg desk or table →

The cable notch ended up facing the room

The top is on back-to-front. Unscrew the legs, rotate the top, re-screw. Twenty minutes and no harm done — the holes are symmetrical on nearly every desk. Full guide: Four-leg desk or table →

There is a step where the two tops meet

The corner plate was tightened with a gap or at different heights. Loosen the plate, press both tops down flat onto a level surface, hold them flush, and retighten from the center outward. Full guide: L-shaped or corner desk →

The desk only touches one wall

Either the desk corner is not square — use the three-four-five check — or your room corner is not square, which is very common. If the desk is square, leave the gap. Full guide: L-shaped or corner desk →

The corner rocks

The shared corner leg is not bearing weight. Adjust its leveling foot down until it does, then recheck the other legs. Full guide: L-shaped or corner desk →

I built it the wrong way round

On most reversible desks, undo the corner plate and the legs on one section, swap the tops, and rebuild. Annoying but no damage done. Check for asymmetric cable notches first. Full guide: L-shaped or corner desk →

The long side sags in the middle

Unsupported span. Move heavy items over the legs, or add a center leg — many manufacturers sell one, and a simple adjustable furniture leg from a hardware store also works. Full guide: L-shaped or corner desk →

The desk does not move at all

Do the initialization reset: hold the down button for about ten seconds until the desk drops slightly and beeps. This is required on a new desk and is the most common 'faulty desk' that is not faulty. Full guide: Electric standing desk →

It makes a beeping error code

Look the code up in the manual — usually it means one motor cable is not fully seated, or the legs are out of sync. Unplug, reseat both motor cables, plug in, and reset. Full guide: Electric standing desk →

One side rises faster than the other

The legs are out of sync. Run the reset procedure, which re-zeroes both motors together. Full guide: Electric standing desk →

A screw came through the desktop

Wrong screw length. Back it out, fill with a wax repair stick in a matching color, and use the short screws. Compare screw length to top thickness before driving any others. Full guide: Electric standing desk →

The desk wobbles at standing height

Some wobble at full extension is normal for single-motor frames. Check the crossbar bolts and foot bolts are fully tight, make sure the frame is centered, and avoid leaning on the far corner. Full guide: Electric standing desk →

A cable got pulled out when it raised

Not enough slack. Unclip the cables, raise the desk to full height, then re-clip with the cables relaxed at that height. Full guide: Electric standing desk →

Living room

The doors do not line up and the gap is crooked

This is what hinge adjustment screws are for, and it is almost never a manufacturing fault. Close both doors, find the uneven gap, and turn the sideways-adjustment screw on the offending hinge a quarter turn at a time. Also confirm the cabinet is square. Full guide: TV stand or media console →

The top sags in the middle under the TV

The TV's weight is over an unsupported span. Shift the TV so its feet sit over the vertical dividers, or add a support block or a center leg underneath. Full guide: TV stand or media console →

A door hangs open or swings shut by itself

The whole unit is not level. Adjust the feet or shim the low side — doors are a very sensitive level gauge. Full guide: TV stand or media console →

The cable holes are in the wrong places for my setup

Most back panels are thin hardboard. A new hole can be cut with a hole saw or even a sharp knife, from the inside out, with the panel supported. Mark it first and keep it away from the edges. Full guide: TV stand or media console →

My TV is wider than the stand

Overhang up to a few inches each side is usually fine structurally but looks wrong and is easy to knock. Check the TV's foot spacing against the stand's top width — feet must land fully on the surface. Full guide: TV stand or media console →

Two sections will not clip together

They are at different heights. Have someone lift one section an inch so the hook can drop into the slot. Also check the legs are all screwed in the same amount. Full guide: Sofa, loveseat or sectional →

There is a gap or a step between sections

Level the legs. One leg not threaded fully in tilts a whole section and shows up as a step at the join. Full guide: Sofa, loveseat or sectional →

The back will not slide into the frame channels

You most likely have the wrong back on that section, or it is upside down. The hooks always point downward into the channel. Full guide: Sofa, loveseat or sectional →

A leg will not screw in straight

Cross-threaded. Back it all the way out, start it again by hand very gently, and feel for the threads catching properly before applying any force. Full guide: Sofa, loveseat or sectional →

The sofa creaks when someone sits down

Usually a loose leg or an unseated clip. Tighten every leg by hand, then lift and reseat each join. Full guide: Sofa, loveseat or sectional →

The cushion covers look loose and wrinkled

Work the foam corners into the cover corners, then zip. New covers are cut to relax into shape and will look right within a week of use. Full guide: Sofa, loveseat or sectional →

It will not fold flat

The mechanism is almost certainly mirrored wrong on one side, or the back deck is in the seat position. Loosen everything and compare both sides against the manual photo. Full guide: Futon or convertible sofa bed →

One side folds and the other sticks

The two mechanisms are at slightly different positions on the deck rails. Loosen both, align them by measuring from the same end, and retighten. Full guide: Futon or convertible sofa bed →

The frame creaks loudly

Barrel bolts have settled. A full tightening lap in the sofa position fixes most of it. Felt or nylon washers at the pivot points kill the rest. Full guide: Futon or convertible sofa bed →

The mattress slides off when I convert it

Use the straps. If none came, luggage straps or heavy-duty velcro ties around the deck slats work perfectly. Full guide: Futon or convertible sofa bed →

A bolt is too short to reach

You have a bolt from the wrong bag, or the frame needs loosening elsewhere so it can shuffle. Loosen everything, let the frame find its shape, then try again. Full guide: Futon or convertible sofa bed →

The table rocks on my floor

Find the leg that lifts, then either screw out its leveling foot or double up a felt pad underneath. Most floors are not flat, so this is normal and permanent. Full guide: Coffee table, end table or console →

The table racks sideways when I push it

The lower shelf or stretcher is loose or missing. That part is the brace. Tighten it, or check the parts list to see if you skipped it. Full guide: Coffee table, end table or console →

One leg sticks out at a different angle

An angled leg plate is mounted the wrong way round. Unscrew it, rotate it so the lean matches the other three, re-screw. Full guide: Coffee table, end table or console →

The glass top slides around

The rubber or suction pads are missing, dirty, or not stuck down. Clean the glass and the frame with glass cleaner, apply fresh pads, lower the glass straight down. Full guide: Coffee table, end table or console →

A screw went through the top

Wrong length screw. Back it out, fill with a colored wax repair stick, and use the short screws. Always compare screw length to top thickness first. Full guide: Coffee table, end table or console →

Dining

The chair wobbles and will not stop

Loosen everything, stand the chair on a flat floor, press hard on the seat, and retighten in a diagonal pattern. Nine times out of ten it was tightened while upside down and the joints settled crooked. Full guide: Dining chairs and stools →

One leg is shorter than the others

Usually it is not — the chair is just assembled slightly out of square. Try the retightening trick first. If it genuinely is short, double up a felt pad. Full guide: Dining chairs and stools →

A bolt spins and will not tighten

The threaded insert in the wood has pulled loose. Pull it out, add a drop of two-part epoxy around it, push it back and let it cure overnight before bolting. Full guide: Dining chairs and stools →

The back leans to one side

Loosen the back bolts, hold it straight while looking from the front, and retighten evenly, alternating sides. Full guide: Dining chairs and stools →

The chair creaks when I sit

A joint is slightly loose. Creaking is wood or metal moving against wood. Find it by sitting and shifting while a helper listens, then tighten that bolt. Full guide: Dining chairs and stools →

Outdoor

A bolt is already rusting

Replace it with a stainless steel bolt of the same size from a hardware store, and grease the thread. Unlike bunk beds, outdoor furniture bolts are usually generic and safe to substitute like for like. Full guide: Patio and outdoor furniture →

The chair rocks on my patio

Check the frame itself on flat ground first. If the frame is fine, the patio is uneven — use adjustable glides or a thin shim under the short leg. Full guide: Patio and outdoor furniture →

The glass top will not sit flat

A rubber pad is missing or out of position, or there is grit on one. Lift the glass with two people, clean everything, reposition the pads, lower straight down. Full guide: Patio and outdoor furniture →

A bolt is seized and will not undo

Penetrating oil, left overnight, then try again. If the head is rounded off, a bolt extractor or a hacksaw cut is the next step. Grease on assembly prevents all of this. Full guide: Patio and outdoor furniture →

The cushions keep blowing away

Use the straps underneath. If the cushions have none, outdoor velcro strips or a few cushion clips solve it permanently. Full guide: Patio and outdoor furniture →

A leg filled with water and split

The glide cap was missing. Replace the glide, and check every other leg. Aluminum splits when water inside freezes, and that damage is not repairable. Full guide: Patio and outdoor furniture →

Nursery

The bolt will not go into the barrel nut

The barrel nut is rotated the wrong way. Put a flat-head screwdriver in the slot on its end and turn it until you can see daylight through its threaded hole when you sight down the bolt hole. Hold it there while you hand-start the bolt. This is the answer about nine times out of ten. Full guide: Convertible crib →

The barrel nut keeps falling out of its hole

Hold the crib end flat so the hole faces up and gravity keeps the nut seated, or put a small piece of tape over the outside of the hole while you start the bolt. Some people use a magnetic screwdriver to hold it in position. Full guide: Convertible crib →

A bolt spins forever and never tightens

Either the barrel nut is rotating in its hole, or the thread has stripped. Try holding the barrel nut still with the screwdriver. If it still spins, stop and order a replacement barrel nut — do not leave that joint partly tight. Full guide: Convertible crib →

The frame is twisted and rocks on the floor

Loosen every bolt a couple of turns, stand the crib on a flat floor, press down on each corner so it settles square, then re-tighten in two laps. Tightening a crib while it sits on carpet or an uneven floor locks the twist in. Full guide: Convertible crib →

I have lost the instructions

Check the pouch sewn to the mattress support — that is what it is for. Otherwise every major manufacturer has manuals by model number on their site, free. Full guide: Convertible crib →

I am missing a bolt and the store is closed

Wait. Do not substitute. Crib bolts are specified for repeated impact loading and a visually identical hardware-store bolt is not the same part. Every manufacturer ships crib hardware free, usually within a few days. Full guide: Convertible crib →

The crib creaks when the baby moves

A creak is a joint moving. Do a full two-lap re-tighten including the four mattress-support bolts underneath. If it still creaks, find which joint by rocking the crib with a hand on each joint in turn. Full guide: Convertible crib →

It is a hand-me-down and I do not know its history

Check the model against the CPSC recall list, confirm no drop-side (those have been banned since 2011), run the soda-can slat test and the two-finger mattress test, and fully re-tighten every fastener. If any hardware is missing, order it new from the manufacturer rather than using the crib as-is. Full guide: Convertible crib →

I do not have the conversion rail

Conversion rails are model-specific and usually sold separately. Find your model number on the label on the crib end's bottom rail and order the matching rail from the manufacturer. Do not improvise one. Full guide: Crib conversion to toddler bed →

The bolt holes do not line up for the stabilizer bar

Pull gently at both ends of the bar as the manual describes — the locating pins need to seat before the bolts will reach. Also loosen the bolts at the other end so the frame can shuffle. Full guide: Crib conversion to toddler bed →

There is a gap between the guardrail and the mattress

Push the mattress hard against the rail. If a gap remains, your mattress is undersized for the frame. A full-size crib mattress is a regulated size — measure yours against it. Full guide: Crib conversion to toddler bed →

The bed racks side to side

The stabilizer bar is missing, loose, or fitted to only one end. That bar is doing the structural work the removed side used to do. Full guide: Crib conversion to toddler bed →

My child is 13 months and climbing out of the crib

Do not convert yet. Instead drop the mattress to its lowest setting if it is not already, remove anything in the crib that can be used as a step, and consider a sleep sack that makes climbing harder. If they are genuinely climbing out, a floor mattress is safer than a toddler bed before 15 months. Full guide: Crib conversion to toddler bed →

Can I convert back to a crib later?

Yes, if you kept the side panel and its hardware. Reassemble, fully re-tighten everything, and re-run the slat and mattress gap checks — a crib on its third assembly needs a careful going-over. Full guide: Crib conversion to toddler bed →

There is a gap of about an inch at one end

That is too much. Two fingers is the limit and an inch comfortably exceeds it. The mattress is undersized for this crib — replace it with one that meets the full-size dimensions. Do not fill the gap with rolled towels or foam; that has caused deaths. Full guide: Crib mattress fit and safe sleep check →

The mattress fits side to side but not end to end

It is a mini crib or play yard mattress in a full-size crib, or a cut-down import. Measure it against 51 5/8 by 27 1/4 inches and replace if it falls short. Full guide: Crib mattress fit and safe sleep check →

Can I add a mattress topper for comfort?

No. Infant mattresses are firm by design and that firmness is the safety feature. A topper creates a soft surface and raises the sleep height, which also shortens the rail. Full guide: Crib mattress fit and safe sleep check →

The sheet keeps coming off a corner

The sheet is too large for the mattress. Buy one sized for that mattress. A loose sheet that balls up is a genuine suffocation risk, not an inconvenience. Full guide: Crib mattress fit and safe sleep check →

My crib came with a bumper in the bedding set

Throw it away. Padded crib bumpers are banned from sale in the US under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act and remain one of the most recalled items in the category. Do not pass it on. Full guide: Crib mattress fit and safe sleep check →

The mattress has a dip where the baby sleeps

Replace it. A dished mattress is both a soft-surface hazard and a sign the mattress support underneath may be sagging — check those four support bolts while you are at it. Full guide: Crib mattress fit and safe sleep check →

Is a second-hand mattress acceptable?

A crib can safely be second-hand if it checks out. A mattress is a harder call: you cannot see internal compression or hygiene, and firmness degrades. Most guidance is to buy the mattress new even when the crib is handed down. Full guide: Crib mattress fit and safe sleep check →

A leg will not click into place

Something is blocking the detent — usually fabric caught in the hinge. Work the fabric clear and try again. Never use a bassinet with a joint that will not lock. Full guide: Bassinet and bedside sleeper →

The sleep surface looks tilted

Check the floor first with a level app, then the frame's height settings — many have independent adjustment at each end. If you cannot get it close to flat, stop using it. Full guide: Bassinet and bedside sleeper →
